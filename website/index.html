AUDITABILITY & REPORTING PROTOCOL (SECTION IV-F)
  • 1. Complete Score Breakdown: Report composite DFI alongside all 4 sub-scores (D-RS, DB-RS, V-RS, M-RS).
  • 2. Probe Specification: Document exact fuzzy thresholds ($\tau=0.92$), vector $k=5$, and MIA probe versions.
  • 3. Scope Disclosure: Explicitly inventory scanned datasets, live tables, replicas, backups, and exclusions.
  • 4. Point-in-Time Timestamp: DFI is temporal and must be re-evaluated after retraining or ingestion.
  • 5. Cryptographic Report Hash: SHA-256 hash chaining (`previous_hash`) establishes immutable provenance.
RESEARCH LIMITATIONS & SCOPE (SECTION VIII)
  • Empirical Lower Bound: Probes quantify detectable residue; high DFI is not formal proof of absence.
  • Cross-Layer Synergy: Probes currently run independently; multi-surface chained attacks are future work.
  • Weight Recalibration: Default 20/20/30/30 weights are starting governance defaults subject to sector risk.
  • Modality Scope: Current probes calibrated for tabular and text; image/multimodal probes are open work.

Regulatory Alignment (Section VII)

Bridging Law and AI Engineering.
GDPR, DPDP Act, and CCPA.

DFI slots directly into privacy impact assessments and third-party vendor audits, translating legal erasure rights into measurable technical bounds.

GDPR ART. 17

Right to Erasure

Mandates deletion without undue delay. DFI provides forensic proof that personal data has been excised not only from primary tables, but from caches, vector indexes, and model parameters.

INDIA DPDP ACT 2023

Section 12 Compliance

Requires data fiduciaries to erase personal data upon withdrawal of consent. DFI produces a tamper-evident audit artifact verifiable by regulatory inspectors.

CCPA / CPRA

Right to Delete

Extends consumer deletion rights to service providers. DFI enables enterprises to audit third-party vendor LLMs and RAG pipelines via black-box probing without requiring access to weights.
